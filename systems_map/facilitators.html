Guide for facilitators

Participatory Systems Mapping with quantitative variables

Only the facilitator needs this page.

Your role

As a facilitator, your role is subtle but important:

  • Keep an eye on the clock and call out the checkpoints in the table below.
  • Make sure everyone gets to speak, especially the quietest person at the table.
  • When something is unclear, ask: "Could you explain that a bit more?"
  • You are not the expert, and nobody at the table has to be. Your job is to help the group's expertise come out.

You are also the only person who needs to watch the time. Say the time out loud at each step boundary, and treat the checkpoints below as a script you can read from.

Checkpoints

MinutesWhat the group is doingWhat you can say
5What interests you, and choose the problem"Decide now, any of them works"
5Write factors, at least three each"Halfway"
20Place around the centre, merge duplicates, then draw arrows"Merge only if the authors agree"
Later: "Let's draw some arrows before time runs out"
5Finish the arrows, circle the connections you would test"Two minutes left, let's hear what you have"

Falling behind is normal. If you are running late, shorten the writing and the merging, and protect the arrow drawing. The arrows are the part the group cannot do later.

Keep the group quantitative

The exercise is qualitative, but every factor on the map should be something you could in principle measure.

Your second job is to keep nudging the group in that direction.

  • If someone writes a fixed label such as "the health system", ask what part of it could go up or down: funding, waiting time, trust in services.
  • When an arrow is drawn, ask what the two variables would be measured on, and in which direction, so the group says the sign out loud.
  • Ask which two or three connections they would most want to test with data, and what data they would need. That question is the bridge to the rest of the session.
  • A question mark arrow is a result, not a gap. Do not let the group tidy it away.

If the conversation stalls

  • Whose perspective is missing from this map? Whose factor would we have written differently?
  • Where are the boundaries: who, when, where does this problem stop?
  • Which factor here would be the hardest to measure, and what would it take to measure it?

If the group finishes early

Ask each member to name, out loud, which two or three connections they would treat as an interaction to test, on which scale, and what data they would need. That is the paragraph their methods draft needs next.

Handing over

Before the group disperses, ask them to photograph the map and keep it. The plenary works from the maps, and an unfinished map is the normal state of a systems map rather than a sign that something went wrong.

Thank you for taking on this role. We hope you enjoyed the exercise.
