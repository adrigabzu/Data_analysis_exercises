Helpful material to learn more about epidemiology & statistics

A starting list for the quantitative sessions in Health Research Methodology. Everything here is free to read or watch unless marked otherwise, and the items are grouped so that you can find one thing at the level you need rather than reading all of it.

Books

  • Baker, C. (2023), Epidemiology (open textbook) Definitions and worked tables for incidence, prevalence, risk, rate and odds, and the source of the tables used in the lectures. →doi.org/10.21061/epidemiology
  • Advanced Statistics and Machine Learning for Health Research (Department course book) 21 chapters with datasets and code covering inference, regression, splines, survival analysis, missing data, prediction models and reporting standards, written for health researchers at this level. →mlhealthcourse.github.io
  • Coggon, Rose and Barker, Epidemiology for the Uninitiated (BMJ) The classic short introduction to how often diseases occur in different groups of people and why, written for readers with no statistical background. →bmj.com · Epidemiology for the uninitiated

Articles

  • Lesko, Fox and Edwards (2022), A Framework for Descriptive Epidemiology The checklist the session is built on, asking you to state the target population, the outcome, how it was measured and which subgroups matter. →doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwac115
  • Fox et al. (2022), On the Need to Revitalize Descriptive Epidemiology The argument that description has been treated as the lesser half of epidemiology. Read it straight after Lesko to see why the framework matters. American Journal of Epidemiology 191(7) →doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwac056
  • Greenland et al. (2016), Statistical tests, P values, confidence intervals, and power: a guide to misinterpretations Twenty-five specific misreadings of p-values and confidence intervals, each with the correct reading next to it. →doi.org/10.1007/s10654-016-0149-3
  • George, Stead and Ganti (2020), What's the Risk: Differentiating Risk Ratios, Odds Ratios, and Hazard Ratios? A short clinical paper that keeps the three measures apart and shows when each one is the right summary. →doi.org/10.7759/cureus.10047
  • Poon and Feng (2023), Univariate and Multiple Regression Analyses in Medical Research What adjustment buys you in a regression and what it does not, written for readers of results tables. Biometrical Letters 60(1):65–76 →doi.org/10.2478/bile-2023-0005

Videos

Each one is under ten minutes. Press play to watch it here.

    Interactive and reference

    • Seeing Theory, Brown University Two chapters are directly relevant: chapter 3 on probability distributions and chapter 4 on frequentist inference, both with interactive figures. →seeing-theory.brown.edu
    • From Data to Viz A decision tree for choosing a plot, from the type of data you have to the chart that suits it. →data-to-viz.com
    • Common statistical tests are linear models Shows that most of the tests you meet are special cases of one model, which is a shortcut to remembering their assumptions. →lindeloev.github.io/tests-as-linear

    Course material

    • The exercise repository (Data_analysis_exercises) The R scripts that show the operations being performed on a simulated register and survey dataset. Optional self-study, since the course assesses methodology rather than code. →github.com/adrigabzu/Data_analysis_exercises
